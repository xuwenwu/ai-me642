# Pilot Feedback

Use these prompts after the first small-class pilot. They are designed to identify practical blockers, not to evaluate student performance.

## Student Feedback Prompts

Ask students to answer briefly:

1. What step was most confusing?
2. Did you understand what to upload?
3. Did the validation report help you revise your interpretation?
4. Were the thermo plots useful?
5. Did the AI prompt-log form make sense?
6. Did you know when the assignment was successfully submitted?
7. What message, label, or instruction should be clearer?
8. How long did the submission process take after your simulation files were ready?

## Instructor or TA Feedback Prompts

After grading the pilot submissions, record:

1. How many students needed login help?
2. How many submissions were missing required files?
3. How many validation reports had warning or failed status?
4. Which validation warning was most useful?
5. Which warning was noisy or hard to interpret?
6. Was grading faster, slower, or about the same as normal?
7. Which page caused the most friction?
8. Which rubric criterion was hardest to apply?
9. What should be changed before the next real assignment?

## Metrics To Capture

- Number of active student accounts.
- Number of created submissions.
- Number of submitted assignments.
- Number of validation warnings.
- Number of validation failures.
- Number of graded submissions.
- Median instructor review time per submission, estimated.
- Most common support request.

## Decision After Pilot

Choose one:

- Continue with another small assignment.
- Pause for UI/message cleanup.
- Expand to a larger class section.
- Keep as instructor-only review tooling for now.

Recommended threshold before expanding: at least 80% of students can submit without one-on-one workflow help after files are ready.
